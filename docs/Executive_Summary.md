title: "New-Business Segmentation and Sales Staffing: Executive Summary"
subtitle: "Rippling Revenue Strategy & Operations case study (fictional dataset, Feb-2021 to Jan-2022)"
---

**Bottom line.** New-logo ARR is growing mainly because more leads are coming in. Monthly new ARR rose **26%** from the Feb–Jul to the Aug–Oct 2021 average, a **$12.3M annualized run-rate**. Over the same period a smaller share of leads reached a demo, and the lead mix moved toward larger, harder-to-win accounts. I recommend three things:

- fix first touch before buying more pipeline
- organize into **four segments run as three motions**
- staff to **49 roles of required capacity (13 SDR, 27 AE, 9 SC)**, hiring only the gap to current headcount

### Three findings

1. **Leads outran demos: a potential top-of-funnel bottleneck.** Leads grew 27%, but lead→demo fell from **31.2% to 28.2%** (a 10% relative drop), while demo→opp (−1%) and opp→win (−3%) held. The slip is largest in 1–50 (−3.5 pts) and 51–100 (−3.7 pts); Enterprise held. The cause is not proven: SDR capacity, speed-to-lead, lead quality and demo scheduling all fit.
2. **51–100 has the strongest observed revenue yield.** **$895 ARR per lead** vs $575 (101–500) and $548 (501+): it converts like 1–50 (9.6% vs 10.0% lead→win) at **2.5× the ARR per logo**. This is yield, not ROI; there is no cost data.
3. **Growth is moving upmarket, where conversion halves.** 101+ employees produce **57% of ARR**, growing 36% (101–500) and 58% (501+). Lead→win falls to 4.6% and 2.4% and demos per logo rise from 3.7 to 5.6–7.8: complex buying that needs SC support. The 1–10 bucket is shrinking and low value.

### Segmentation

Each boundary sits where the data changes: ARR per logo jumps at 50, conversion halves at 100 and halves again at 500.

| Segment | Motion | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Velocity 1–50 | Velocity | Dedicated SDR + AE; self-scheduling for 1–10; SC by exception |
| Emerging MM 51–100 | Mid-Market | Dedicated SDR + AE; routed first; shared SC pool |
| Core MM 101–500 | Mid-Market | Dedicated SDR + AE; SC on about half of demos |
| Enterprise 501+ | Enterprise | Dedicated SDR + AE + SC on every deal |

Alternatives tested: **1–100 / 101–500 / 501+** mixes $3.0K and $9.3K deals in one AE queue; **1–50 / 51–500 / 501+** mixes 9.6% and 4.6% lead→win in one team.

### Staffing (required capacity at Aug–Oct 2021 demand)

| | 1–50 | 51–100 | 101–500 | 501+ | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SDR | 2 | 2 | 5 | 4 | **13** |
| AE | 4 | 5 | 11 | 7 | **27** |
| SC | 5 (shared pool) | | | 4 | **9** |
| Run-rate ARR | $1.56M | $3.34M | $5.09M | $2.31M | **$12.31M** |

- **Sizing.** Each role is workload ÷ (illustrative capacity × 85% utilization), rounded up. The shared SC pool rounds up the combined need of segments under 500.
- **Not a hiring plan.** Current headcount is unknown, so this is required capacity. Compare it to today's team and hire the gap.
- **Upside sensitivity.** If lead→demo recovers to Feb–Jul levels, demos rise about 36 a month, adding **+$0.86M ARR** and **+1 Core MM AE** (50 roles). This is a scenario, not a forecast.
- **Optional illustrative cost:** about $10.6M a year fully loaded (appendix only; not ROI).

### Recommendations and next 90 days

1. **Diagnose lead→demo before adding pipeline spend or SDRs.** 30 days: instrument speed-to-lead and audit leads that never booked. 60 days: pilot an SLA and self-scheduling in 1–50 and 51–100.
2. **Organize to four segments and three motions.** Route 51–100 leads first.
3. **Staff to required capacity and hire only the gap.** At 90 days, add the upside AE only if lead→demo recovers.
4. **Invest upmarket with discipline.** SC support for 101+, no Enterprise expansion until win rates improve, and no new headcount for 1–10.

**Key limitations:** no headcount or cost data; same-month ratios, not cohorts; one year only, so the Nov–Dec dip is unconfirmed as seasonal; 101–500 is a single wide bucket. All figures reconcile to the Excel model.
