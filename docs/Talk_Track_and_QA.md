# Talk Track (15–20 minutes) and Anticipated Q&A

**Timing plan:**

- About 5 minutes held back for questions.
- The appendix is backup only; pull it up when a question calls for it.

## Cover (0:30)
"Thanks for the time. I treated this as a revenue question: where is new-logo ARR coming from, where is it leaking, and how should SDRs, AEs and SCs be organized to grow it. I'll go through three findings, then segmentation, staffing, and a 90-day plan. The detail is in the appendix and the Excel model, and everything in the deck ties back to it."

## Slide 1: Executive summary (1:30)
"The headline: the business is growing, and most of that growth comes from more leads, not better conversion. Monthly new ARR is up 26% between the first half of the year and August–October, a $12.3 million run-rate.

Three things sit underneath that:
- A smaller share of leads is reaching a demo.
- 51–100 is the best revenue yield we have.
- Growth is moving upmarket, where deals are harder.

So the recommendation is to fix first touch, organize around four segments, and staff to required capacity of 49 roles. Because I don't know the current team, that's a capacity number, not a hiring number."

## Slide 2: Finding 1, leads outran demos (2:30)
"The grey line is leads and the purple line is demos. Leads climbed steadily. Demos barely moved.

Comparing an average month in Feb–Jul with Aug–Oct:
- Lead-to-demo fell from 31.2% to 28.2%, about a 10% relative drop. That's the biggest move at any stage.
- After the demo, things held up much better. Demo-to-opp and win rates moved by only one to three percent relative.

So the leak is at first touch. The slip is largest in 1–50 and 51–100, while Enterprise held. Some of the blended drop is mix, but every segment under 500 declined on its own.

I'm deliberately calling this a *potential* bottleneck. The same pattern fits four explanations:
- not enough SDR capacity
- slower speed-to-lead
- weaker lead quality as marketing scaled
- friction in scheduling demos

Each points to a different fix, which is why the plan starts with a diagnostic rather than a hiring request.

One note on Nov–Dec: leads and conversion both dipped, then January set records. With one year of data I don't treat that as confirmed seasonality, so I left it out of the staffing baseline."

## Slide 3: Finding 2, 51–100 has the best revenue yield (2:00)
"If you divide ARR by leads, 51–100 stands out at $895 per lead, well above any other segment.
- **Why:** it converts like small business, about 9.6% lead-to-win against 10% for 1–50, but each logo is worth $9.3K instead of $3.7K, with the same number of demos per logo.
- **Where it slipped:** it also had the largest lead-to-demo decline.

One caveat I want to be explicit about: this is revenue yield, not ROI. We have no cost data. What I'm confident saying is that a lost lead here costs more ARR than anywhere else, so it's the first place to protect coverage."

## Slide 4: Finding 3, growth is moving upmarket (2:00)
"On the left is ARR growth by segment. Single digits below 100 employees, then 36% in 101–500 and 58% in 501+. Companies over 100 employees are now 57% of ARR, and their share of leads is rising.

But conversion roughly halves at 100 employees and halves again at 500. Demo-to-opp drops and demos per logo double. That's what complex, multi-stakeholder buying looks like, and it's where Solution Consultants earn their place.

At the other end, the 1–10 bucket is shrinking and earns about $3K per logo. It should be served with a low-touch motion, not more people."

## Slide 5: Segmentation (2:00)
"I didn't start from standard SMB/MM/ENT lines. I put a boundary wherever the data changes:
- **At 50:** deal size jumps about two and a half times while conversion stays flat.
- **At 100:** conversion halves.
- **At 500:** it halves again.

That gives four segments, but only three motions. 51–100 and 101–500 share Mid-Market leadership and an SC pool, with separate AE teams and quotas so the higher-yield 51–100 deals aren't crowded out.

I compared two alternatives:
- **1–100 / 101–500 / 501+:** puts $3K and $9K deals in the same AE queue.
- **1–50 / 51–500 / 501+:** mixes two very different conversion profiles in one team.

The tradeoff of my structure is a bit more routing and comp-plan complexity, which I think is worth it."

## Slide 6: Staffing (2:30)
"The method is one line per role:
- SDRs = leads ÷ lead capacity
- AEs = demos ÷ demo capacity
- SCs = demos × an engagement rate ÷ SC capacity

Each is at 85% target utilization and rounded up so the team covers the modeled workload. The capacities are illustrative assumptions, labeled as such and editable in the model.

At Aug–Oct demand that's 13 SDRs, 27 AEs and 9 SCs, 49 roles in total.
- **Shared SC pool:** below 500 employees the SC need is fractional, so one pool of 5 covers all three of those segments.
- **Enterprise:** gets 4 dedicated SCs, close to 2 AEs per SC, because every Enterprise deal needs one.

How this ties to the findings:
- dedicated SDR coverage where lead-to-demo slipped
- the biggest AE team in Core Mid-Market, the largest ARR pool
- SCs concentrated upmarket

Two framing points:
- This is required capacity. We'd compare it to the current team and hire only the gap.
- If lead-to-demo recovers to first-half levels, demos rise about 36 a month, worth roughly $0.9 million of ARR and one more Core MM AE. That's a sensitivity, not a forecast. If the cause turns out to be lead quality, that upside doesn't exist."

## Slide 7: Recommendations and 30/60/90 (2:30)
"Four recommendations.

1. **Fix lead-to-demo before buying more pipeline.**
   - First 30 days: measure speed-to-lead and conversion by segment and source, and audit a sample of leads that never booked. That tells us whether this is capacity, speed or quality.
   - Days 30 to 60: pilot a speed-to-lead SLA and self-scheduling in 1–50 and 51–100 against a control group.
2. **Organize around the four segments** and route 51–100 first.
3. **Staff to required capacity and hire only the gap.** At 90 days we compare current headcount to the model and hire where the pilot showed a lift.
4. **Invest upmarket with discipline.**
   - Put an SC on every Enterprise deal, but don't expand Enterprise headcount until win rates move.
   - Don't add people for 1–10.

Where I'd avoid overinvesting: Enterprise AE expansion, 1–10 headcount, and more lead generation before we understand the first-touch problem.

Happy to take questions."

## Anticipated questions and suggested answers

**1. How do you know the lead-to-demo decline is an SDR capacity problem?**
I don't, and I'm careful not to claim it.
- **What the data shows:** conversion fell most in the months and segments where lead volume grew, and post-demo stages held. That points to the top of the funnel.
- **Competing explanations:** SDR capacity, speed-to-lead, lead quality and demo scheduling all fit the same pattern. Each needs a different fix.
- **How to separate them:** the 30-day diagnostic (response times, lead source mix, an audit of unbooked leads) and the 60-day controlled pilot. That's why I don't put a hiring request ahead of the diagnosis.

**2. Why not just use the standard SMB / MM / Enterprise split?**
Because the data breaks somewhere else.
- **Under 1–100 / 101–500 / 501+:** 51–100 deals ($9.3K per logo) sit in the same queue as 1–10 deals ($3.0K), roughly a 3× spread. That dilutes AE time on the highest-yield segment.
- **What the four-segment version keeps:** each team within one conversion profile and one deal-size band.
- **The cost:** more routing rules and quota plans. I limit it by running only three motions and sharing the SC pool.

**3. Where do the capacity assumptions come from? Aren't they made up?**
- **What they are:** illustrative planning assumptions, labeled as such. They're not Rippling data, because the dataset has no headcount or activity data.
- **Why they're still reasonable:** they reflect reasonable workloads, and they scale with complexity the data does show. Demo-to-opp falls and demos per logo rise in larger segments.
- **Why that's OK:** the model makes each one a single editable cell. The first step in a real engagement would be calibrating them against rep-level activity data.
- **Most sensitive input:** AE demo capacity. ±20% moves the AE requirement from 22 to 34 against a base of 27.

**4. How many people should we hire?**
I can't answer that from this data, and I'd be wary of anyone who did. The model gives required capacity, 49 roles at Aug–Oct demand. Hiring need = required capacity − current ramped headcount + expected attrition. The workbook has blank cells to enter current headcount and see the gap immediately.

**5. Is 51–100 your most profitable segment?**
- **What I can say:** it has the highest observed ARR per lead, $895, because it converts like small business at about 2.5× the deal size.
- **What I can't say:** that it's the highest ROI. There's no cost, retention or sales-cycle data.
- **Why it's still a fair priority:** it's where a lost lead costs the most ARR, and it had the biggest lead-to-demo slip.

**6. Why exclude November and December?**
- **What happened:** leads fell about 47% and every conversion rate dipped, then January was a record.
- **Why I don't call it seasonal:** with one year of data I can't confirm it.
- **Why it's out of the baseline:** the team has to be sized for normal months. Averaging in a trough would leave it short most of the year.
- **The implication:** if the dip does recur, annual ARR will come in below the ×12 run-rate and the team will have slack in Q4. That slack is a good window for hiring and onboarding.

**7. Enterprise is growing fastest. Why not invest more there?**
- **The case for:** it is growing fastest, 58% ARR growth, and has the largest deals.
- **The case against, for now:** lead-to-win is 2.4%, it takes about 41 leads per logo, and it's the most expensive coverage to build.
- **What I recommend:** fund Enterprise to its current required capacity, put an SC on every deal, and track win rate. Expand headcount once win rates or deal sizes improve. Its lead-to-demo didn't decline, so its constraint is winning, not first touch.

**8. Why does the upside add only one AE but $0.86M of ARR?**
- **Headcount:** recovering lead-to-demo adds about 36 demos a month across three segments. Rounding up the baseline already left headroom (AE utilization is 71–83%), so only Core MM crosses into a 12th AE.
- **SDRs:** they don't change because they're sized to leads, and leads don't change in this scenario.
- **Revenue:** that's also why I present the $0.86M as a sensitivity. It depends on the cause of the decline, not on the headcount.

**9. What would change your recommendation?**
- **Lead quality:** if the diagnostic shows the decline is lead quality rather than follow-up, I'd shift effort to marketing and lead scoring and drop the upside.
- **Current headcount:** if it is already above required capacity, the answer is productivity and routing, not hiring.
- **Cost or retention data:** if it showed upmarket deals pay back much faster, I'd lean harder into Core MM and Enterprise.
- **Finer data inside 101–500:** a break near 250 employees could justify splitting that segment.

**10. How would you measure success?**
- **Leading indicators:**
  - speed-to-lead
  - lead-to-demo by segment (target: back toward the Feb–Jul levels of about 39% in 1–100 and 27.5% in 101–500)
  - demo show rate
- **Lagging indicators:**
  - opp-to-win by segment
  - ARR per AE
  - new-logo ARR against the $12.3M run-rate

These would be reviewed monthly by segment so we can see whether each motion is working.
